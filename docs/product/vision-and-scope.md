# Vision and scope

## Promise

Make a small sleeping world sing through understandable spatial puzzles. The player holds and rearranges light-routing pieces, sees the effect immediately, and restores a miniature tree one branch at a time.

Audience: people who enjoy calm logic puzzles and tactile miniature worlds. Sessions are seated and self-paced. Musical knowledge and fast reflexes are unnecessary.

## Design pillars

| Pillar | Observable consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Think with your hands | Pick up, place, and turn pieces; generous automatic alignment. |
| Understand the light | Pulse position and timing are visible without sound or color alone. |
| Restore something | Each solution permanently contributes to the tree's visual and musical growth. |
| Finish a satisfying moment | One early puzzle can be completed within a short session. |
| Stay small enough to polish | One setting, three piece types, six authored puzzles. |

## Baseline scope

One miniature tree, a fixed seated viewpoint with a rotatable board, six ordered puzzles, a contextual tutorial, replay, undo, progressive hints, pause/resume, local progress, sound controls, reduced motion, and contrast options.

The submitted experience must be fully operable with hands, including navigation and settings. Desktop controls and XR emulation support development and access.

No multiplayer, remote accounts, server API, AI service, procedural level generation, user level editor, monetization, room scanning, or persistent world anchors in the baseline. No full locomotion or high-speed hand challenges.

## Success criteria

The first implementation gate is a complete two-flower puzzle that produces consistent results across supported input adapters. Subsequent targets are a first bloom within 60 seconds of playable entry, understanding without a text tutorial wall, recoverable mistakes, and a full progression that supports several short sessions.

These are design targets, not measured usability claims. The final release needs the evidence in the [test plan](../engineering/testing-and-performance.md).

## What makes this spatial

Paths cross at different heights. Looking around and rotating the miniature reveals connections; the board rotation is available to avoid leaning. Piece placement is embodied, while the growth reward surrounds the small work area.

A routing puzzle is not new by itself. Our proposed distinction is timing synchronization combined with a growing musical sculpture. The prototype must establish that combination's value.

## Scope cuts

If time slips, reduce decorative species, musical layers, and nonessential effects first. Keep six small puzzles if viable; prefer fewer fully verified puzzles to broken content, and revise submission claims if the release count changes. Never cut the hand-accessible menu path, recovery, or evidence honesty.
